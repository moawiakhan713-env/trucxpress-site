Dry Van Dispatch Service

Dry van is the backbone of American trucking: retail goods, packaged food, paper, consumer products and almost anything else that travels on pallets. That volume is good news for owner-operators, but it also means competition. A dry van truck makes money when it stays on good lanes with short empty legs between loads. That is what our dispatchers plan for.

What dry van freight looks like

  • Equipment: a 53-foot enclosed trailer, usually 102 inches wide, with swing or roll-up doors.
  • Capacity: about 26 standard pallets loaded single-stacked, and roughly 42,000 to 45,000 pounds of cargo depending on your tractor and trailer weight.
  • Common shippers: distribution centers, manufacturers, retailers and grocery suppliers.
  • Load types: live load and unload, drop and hook, and multi-stop loads.

How we dispatch dry van trucks

  1. Lane planning first. We find out where you want to run and where home is, then target freight markets that keep you moving in that loop.
  2. Rate negotiation on every load. We compare the offer to current lane rates and push back before you commit.
  3. Drop and hook when it pays. Drop trailers save hours at docks. We favor them when the rate is comparable.
  4. Detention tracked. We note in and out times so detention gets billed instead of forgotten.
  5. Reload before delivery. The next load is booked while you are still rolling, not after you are empty.

Dry van rates: what moves them

Rates follow supply and demand on each lane. Freight leaving strong manufacturing and port markets usually pays better than freight leaving consumer markets that mostly receive goods. Volume climbs ahead of retail seasons and around month and quarter ends. We watch these swings and adjust your lanes, rather than letting you sit in a market that has gone soft.

Getting more out of every dry van load

  • Accessorials: stop-off pay, detention, layover and TONU are negotiated up front, not argued about later.
  • Driver assist vs no-touch: we confirm whether you are expected to help unload and price it in.
  • Lumper receipts: kept and submitted so you are reimbursed.
  • Seals and counts: we remind you to record seal numbers and note shortages on the paperwork before you leave.

Equipment details brokers ask about

Brokers sometimes ask whether your trailer has air ride, logistics posts or e-track, load locks or straps, and whether it is food grade. Some freight requires a clean, odor-free trailer. Telling us your setup lets us filter loads you can actually take.

Frequently asked questions

Can you dispatch a dry van with a new MC?

Yes. Dry van has the most brokers willing to work with newer authorities. See our page on dispatch for new authority.

Do you book multi-stop loads?

Yes, when the rate covers the extra time. We confirm stop count, appointment times and stop-off pay before booking.

Do I have to run a set lane?

No. You choose your preferred regions and home time. We build the plan around your rules and you approve every load.

Do you book food grade dry van loads?

Yes, when your trailer is clean and meets the shipper's requirements. We confirm the shipper's conditions before booking.
