The Arizona freight market in plain terms

Phoenix is one of the fastest-growing metro areas in the country, and growing cities consume more than they ship. That means more freight comes into Arizona than goes out. Getting into Phoenix is usually easy and pays well. Getting out is where carriers lose money, with lower outbound rates and trucks sitting empty waiting for a load.

Good Arizona dispatch is mostly about the second half of the trip: planning the outbound load before you deliver the inbound one.

Corridors we plan around

  • I-10 links Los Angeles, Phoenix, Tucson and El Paso, and carries a large share of the state's freight both ways.
  • I-17 and I-40 connect Phoenix to Flagstaff and the east-west traffic across northern Arizona.
  • I-19 to Nogales, one of the busiest crossings for fresh produce from Mexico, especially in the winter growing season.
  • I-8 toward Yuma and San Diego, important for winter vegetables out of the Yuma area.

Seasonal patterns that change rates

  • Winter produce (roughly November to April): Nogales and Yuma generate strong reefer demand. Reefer carriers can find better outbound options in these months.
  • Summer heat: extreme temperatures raise reefer demand and make pre-cooling and temperature records more important.
  • Holiday retail (fall): inbound dry van volume into Phoenix distribution centers rises, so inbound rates firm up.

How we dispatch Arizona carriers

  1. We learn your home base, equipment and how often you want to be home.
  2. Before you deliver, we line up the next load so you are not stuck searching Phoenix load boards on a Friday afternoon.
  3. When outbound rates are weak, we compare a short repositioning move to a nearby market against waiting, and pick the option that earns more for the week.
  4. We vet every broker and handle setup packets, rate confirmations and check calls.

Conditions Arizona drivers plan for

  • Summer heat above 110°F in Phoenix is hard on tires, cooling systems and batteries, and on drivers waiting at docks.
  • Dust storms can drop visibility to near zero, especially along I-10 between Phoenix and Tucson during monsoon season. If caught in one, pull off as far as possible, turn off your lights and keep your foot off the brake.
  • Winter snow around Flagstaff on I-17 and I-40 can close lanes or require chains.

We factor these into timing, so a delivery appointment does not collide with a storm or a closed highway.

Frequently asked questions

Do you only dispatch trucks based in Arizona?

No. We dispatch carriers across the country. Arizona-based carriers benefit from our local knowledge of Phoenix and Tucson freight.

Is it hard to find loads out of Phoenix?

It can be. More freight comes into Phoenix than leaves, so outbound rates are often lower. Planning the outbound load early, and sometimes repositioning a short distance, is how we keep the round trip profitable.

Which equipment does well in Arizona?

Dry van has the most volume. Reefer does well during the winter produce season out of Nogales and Yuma. Flatbed and step deck follow construction and solar projects across the state.

Running out of Arizona?

Tell us your home base and lanes and we will plan loads that bring you back with money in the truck.
