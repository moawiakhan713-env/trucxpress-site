Step Deck Dispatch Service

A step deck, also called a drop deck, has an upper deck over the tractor and a lower main deck behind it. That lower deck lets you haul taller freight without an over-height permit. Fewer trucks can carry these loads than standard flatbed freight, which often means better rates for step deck carriers.

How step deck dimensions work

A common 48-foot step deck has an upper deck of about 10 to 11 feet and a lower deck of about 37 to 38 feet. The lower deck sits closer to the ground, so freight up to roughly 10 feet tall can usually move without an over-height permit, compared with about 8.5 feet on a standard flatbed. Exact numbers depend on your trailer, so we always ask for your deck lengths and heights.

Loads that suit a step deck

  • Tractors, excavators and other machinery that can drive on with ramps
  • Tall industrial equipment and HVAC units
  • Agricultural equipment
  • Building materials and freight that is too tall for a flatbed

What we check before booking

  • Exact dimensions: height, width, length and weight of every piece, and where it sits on the deck.
  • Loading method: forklift, crane or driving on with ramps. Ramp loads need ramps on your trailer.
  • Securement and tarps: the gear required and any tarp pay.
  • Permits: anything over legal width or height, and who arranges permits and escorts.

Step deck, flatbed or RGN?

  • Flatbed: the most common open deck, with a flat surface around 5 feet off the ground. Best for freight up to about 8.5 feet tall.
  • Step deck: a lower main deck lets freight up to about 10 feet tall stay within legal height in most states.
  • RGN (removable gooseneck): the front detaches so equipment drives on from the front, and the deck sits even lower. Used for heavy and very tall machinery.

Knowing where your trailer fits keeps you on loads you can haul legally and profitably.

Keeping a step deck busy

Step deck freight comes in waves: equipment dealers, auctions, construction season and agricultural seasons all move machinery. Between those, many step deck carriers take standard flatbed loads to avoid sitting. We keep both options open and book whichever pays better for the week.

Frequently asked questions

Can a step deck haul regular flatbed freight?

Yes. Step decks often take standard flatbed loads too, which gives you more options in slow weeks. We book those when they pay well enough.

Do I need ramps?

Not for every load, but ramps open up drive-on machinery freight that usually pays more.

Do you book loads that need permits?

Yes, when your truck is set up for them. We confirm who handles and pays for permits and escorts before you accept.

What is the maximum legal height on a step deck?

Most states set a legal overall height of 13 feet 6 inches, with some allowing 14 feet or more. On a typical step deck lower deck, that leaves room for freight of about 10 feet. Anything taller usually needs a permit.
