Reefer Dispatch Service

Refrigerated freight usually pays more than dry van, and it asks more of the carrier: tighter appointments, temperature records, pre-cooling and claims risk if anything goes wrong. A good reefer dispatcher reads every load detail before you accept, so the higher rate does not come with a surprise.

What reefer loads include

  • Fresh produce: fruit and vegetables, often loaded at farms and packing sheds with long wait times.
  • Frozen food: set points around -10°F to 0°F for frozen goods, usually with tighter appointments.
  • Chilled food and dairy: meat, dairy and beverages at controlled cool temperatures.
  • Non-food: some pharmaceuticals, chemicals, flowers and other temperature-sensitive goods.

What we check before you accept a reefer load

  • Set point and mode: the exact temperature and whether the unit should run continuous or cycle.
  • Pre-cool requirement: how cold the trailer must be when you back into the door.
  • Pulp temperature: for produce, we remind you to check and record product temperature at pickup.
  • Appointment and wait times: produce sheds can hold trucks for hours. We price that into the decision.
  • Lumper and unloading fees: who pays, and how you get reimbursed.
  • Washout requirements: some receivers and food shippers require a clean trailer certificate.

Seasonal planning

Reefer demand moves with growing seasons. Winter brings strong outbound volume from the Southwest, including Nogales and Yuma. Spring and summer shift volume toward the Southeast, California's Central Valley and later the Midwest and Northwest. We plan your weeks around where produce is moving so you are near the freight when rates are strongest.

What brokers expect from a reefer carrier

  • A reliable reefer unit that is serviced on schedule, since a breakdown can spoil a full load.
  • Temperature records you can download or print if a receiver questions the load.
  • Reefer breakdown coverage on your cargo policy, which many brokers ask for.
  • Food safety habits: clean trailers, washouts when required, and following the shipper's loading instructions.

Why use a reefer dispatcher

Reefer loads come with more fine print than dry freight: temperatures, appointment windows, lumpers, washouts and seasonal timing. A dispatcher who reads every detail before you accept keeps the higher reefer rate from turning into a long wait, a fee you did not plan for, or a claim.

Frequently asked questions

Do you handle multi-temp or frozen loads?

Yes, if your unit is rated for it. We match the load's set point and requirements to your equipment before booking.

How do you protect me from temperature claims?

We confirm the set point and mode in writing on the rate confirmation, remind you to pre-cool and record pulp temperatures, and keep the shipper and receiver times documented.

Can a dry van carrier switch to reefer?

Yes, with a reefer trailer and the right insurance coverage, including reefer breakdown coverage that many brokers ask for. We can explain what brokers expect before you invest.

What happens if a receiver rejects my reefer load?

Report it to your dispatcher and the broker immediately, keep the temperature records and paperwork, and do not leave without instructions. Documentation from pickup onward is what protects you.
