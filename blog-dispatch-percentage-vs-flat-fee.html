Dispatch Percentage vs Flat Fee: Which Costs Less for One Truck?

Owner-operators ask me this question more than any other: should I pay my dispatcher a percentage or a flat weekly fee? The honest answer depends on how much your truck grosses and how steady your weeks are. Here is the math, with example numbers you can swap for your own, including a case where the flat fee wins.

MK
By Moawia KhanFounder & CEO, TrucXpress

The two models

  • Percentage: the dispatcher takes a share of each load's rate. Many charge between 5% and 10%.
  • Flat weekly fee: a fixed amount per truck per week, no matter how much the truck earns.

Example: a strong week

Say your truck grosses $8,000 in a good week. These numbers are illustrations, not quotes.

Percentage at 6%Flat fee at $300
Dispatch cost$480$300
DifferenceFlat saves $180

In a strong week, a flat fee looks cheaper.

Example: a slow week

Now the truck breaks down for two days and grosses $3,000.

Percentage at 6%Flat fee at $300
Dispatch cost$180$300
DifferencePercentage saves $120

And if the truck sits the whole week for repairs, the percentage costs nothing while the flat fee still charges $300.

What the math leaves out

  • Incentives. A flat-fee dispatcher earns the same whether your rate is $2.10 or $2.70. A percentage dispatcher earns more when you earn more, so they push harder on every negotiation.
  • Cash flow. A one-truck operation feels a fixed fee most in its worst weeks, exactly when cash is shortest.
  • Hidden extras. Some flat-fee services add setup, software or per-packet fees. Compare total cost, not the headline number.

Find your break-even week

Divide the flat fee by the percentage. At $300 and 6%, the break-even is $5,000 of gross revenue per week ($300 ÷ 0.06). Above that, the flat fee costs fewer dollars. Below it, the percentage does.

A full year, honestly

Take one truck with 40 strong weeks at $7,500, 8 slow weeks at $3,000 and 4 weeks off for repairs and home time. These are example numbers.

Percentage at 6%Flat fee at $300
40 strong weeks$18,000$12,000
8 slow weeks$1,440$2,400
4 weeks off$0$1,200
Year total$19,440$15,600

In this example the flat fee costs less over the year. Lower the strong-week gross, add more downtime, or start a new authority with uneven weeks, and the percentage comes out ahead.

Which one should you choose?

Run your own numbers with the break-even rule. If you reliably gross well above it every week, a flat fee can cost fewer dollars. If your weeks are uneven, you are new, or you want your dispatcher paid only when you are, a percentage is the safer choice: you never pay in a week the truck sits, and the dispatcher earns more only when your rates go up. That risk-sharing is why TrucXpress works on commission only.

Frequently asked questions

Is 10% too much for a dispatcher?

It depends on what is included. A 10% fee with broker vetting, paperwork, invoicing support and 24/7 coverage can be fair. A 10% fee for posting your truck on a load board is not.

Do percentage dispatchers charge on fuel surcharge?

Some do. Always ask whether the percentage applies to the line-haul rate only or the total including fuel and accessorials.

Can I switch from flat fee to percentage later?

With TrucXpress there is no long-term contract, so you can change your setup any time.
