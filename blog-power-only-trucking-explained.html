Power Only Trucking: How It Works and Who It Suits

Power only is the simplest way to put a tractor to work: you supply the truck and driver, and the shipper or logistics company supplies the trailer, usually already loaded. It has grown quickly, with large shippers and logistics platforms such as Amazon Relay running big power only programs. Our sales team gets more questions about it every month.

How it works

  1. You accept a power only load from a broker, 3PL or shipper program.
  2. You hook a preloaded trailer at a shipper, drop yard or distribution center.
  3. You deliver and drop the trailer, often picking up another one nearby.

Most power only freight is drop and hook, so you spend far less time waiting at docks.

Who it suits

  • New owner-operators who want one equipment payment instead of two
  • Carriers between trailers while one is in the shop
  • Fleets with extra tractors
  • Drivers who prefer drop and hook over live loading

What you need

  • Active authority and insurance meeting the program's limits
  • Trailer interchange insurance, which covers damage to a trailer you do not own. Standard liability does not.
  • A clean inspection habit. Inspect every trailer before you pull it and photograph any damage.

The money side

Power only rates are often a bit lower than the same lane with your own trailer, since you are not supplying equipment. You save the trailer payment, insurance and maintenance, and drop and hook lets you fit more loads into a week. The best results come from loops where the next trailer is close to where you dropped the last one.

Inspect every trailer before you pull it

You are responsible for a trailer you do not own, so a quick inspection protects you:

  1. Walk around and photograph every side, the roof edge and the doors.
  2. Check tires, lights, brakes, mud flaps and air lines.
  3. Confirm the seal number matches the paperwork.
  4. Note any existing damage on the paperwork before you leave the yard.

Common power only mistakes

  • Running without trailer interchange coverage, which leaves you paying for any damage.
  • Ignoring trailer due dates, since some programs charge if a trailer is not returned on time.
  • Chasing far-away trailers, when the empty miles to hook cancel out the rate.

Frequently asked questions

Do I need a trailer for power only?

No. That is the point. You need a tractor that can pull standard trailers.

Is trailer interchange insurance expensive?

It is usually an add-on to your policy. The cost depends on the coverage amount the program requires.

Can I do power only with a new MC?

Some programs accept newer carriers and some require a few months of history. We check requirements for you before you apply.

Is power only good for new owner-operators?

It is one of the easiest ways to start: no trailer payment, fewer dock delays and simpler operations. The trade-off is slightly lower rates on some lanes.
