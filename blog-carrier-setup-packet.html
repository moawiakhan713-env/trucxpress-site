What Is a Carrier Setup Packet? Everything Brokers Ask For

Before a broker hands you a load, it needs to know you are a real, insured, authorized carrier and how to pay you. That is the carrier setup packet. On our dispatch desk we see good loads lost every week because a carrier's packet took two hours instead of ten minutes. Here is what goes in it.

The documents in a typical setup packet

  1. Operating authority. Your MC or USDOT authority letter showing you are authorized to haul for hire.
  2. Certificate of insurance (COI). Proof of auto liability and cargo coverage. Brokers usually want to be listed as the certificate holder, and they often request the COI straight from your insurance agent.
  3. W-9. Your business tax ID so the broker can pay and report payments.
  4. Broker-carrier agreement. The contract that sets payment terms, liability and rules like no re-brokering. Read it before you sign.
  5. Payment details. A voided check or ACH form, or a Notice of Assignment if you use a factoring company.
  6. Contact and company details. Dispatch contact, after-hours number, equipment list and sometimes driver information.

Many brokers now use online onboarding platforms that verify your identity, authority and insurance automatically. Keep your FMCSA registration, phone and email consistent everywhere, because mismatches slow approval or get you rejected.

How to get approved fast

  • Keep a ready-to-send folder with your authority letter, W-9, voided check or NOA, and a recent COI.
  • Save your insurance agent's contact so certificates can be sent to brokers the same hour.
  • Know your numbers: USDOT, MC, EIN, insurance policy numbers and limits.
  • Answer verification calls. Some brokers call the number on your FMCSA record. If nobody answers, setup stalls.

Read the broker-carrier agreement

A few clauses deserve a careful look:

  • Payment terms and any quick-pay fees
  • Detention, layover and TONU (truck ordered, not used) terms
  • Claims and cargo liability limits and deadlines
  • Non-solicitation clauses that stop you from working directly with the broker's shippers

Why setups get rejected

  • The phone number or email does not match the FMCSA record
  • Insurance limits are below what the broker requires
  • The certificate of insurance does not list the broker as certificate holder
  • The authority is still pending or inactive
  • The bank details do not match the business name

Most of these are fixed in minutes once you know about them, which is why we check them before submitting.

Frequently asked questions

Do I need a new packet for every broker?

Yes. Each broker has its own agreement and onboarding, though the documents you send are mostly the same. Online platforms save time with brokers who use them.

Why does the broker want to be the certificate holder?

It lets the broker confirm your coverage directly with your insurance agent and receive notice if the policy is cancelled.

Can my dispatcher complete packets for me?

Yes. At TrucXpress we fill out and submit packets for our carriers, and only you sign the agreements.

How long does broker setup take?

With documents ready, many setups are approved the same day. Missing insurance certificates and verification calls are the usual delays.
