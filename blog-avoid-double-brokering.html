5 Red Flags of Double Brokering and How to Avoid the Scam

Double brokering, when a load is re-brokered without permission, is one of the fastest-growing scams in trucking. The carrier does the work and the middleman disappears with the money. On our dispatch desk we check for these signs on every single load, and you can too.

How the scam works

A fraudster poses as a broker or a carrier, books freight from a legitimate broker, then re-posts it to you, often at an attractive rate. You haul the load, but your rate confirmation is with someone who never intended to pay. Weeks later you are chasing an invoice from a company that no longer answers the phone, and the real broker says it already paid the "carrier."

The five red flags

  1. The rate is too good. Scammers fill trucks fast by posting above-market rates. If a load pays well over the lane average, slow down and verify.
  2. A brand-new or mismatched broker. An authority that is only weeks old, or an email domain that does not match the company on the rate confirmation, is a serious warning.
  3. Pressure to skip paperwork. Legitimate brokers expect setup packets and insurance checks. Anyone rushing you to "just book it" is hiding something.
  4. Pickup paperwork names someone else. If the shipper's bill of lading lists a different broker or carrier than the one you booked with, the load may have been re-brokered. Call before you load.
  5. Payment details keep changing. Vague answers about factoring, a new remit-to address or refusing to confirm terms in writing all point to trouble.

How we block it at TrucXpress

Every load we present goes through broker vetting first:

  • Authority and bond check on the FMCSA public records
  • Credit and payment history review
  • Contact verification, matching phone numbers and email domains to the broker's official listing
  • Rate confirmation review against the load details before you sign

What to do if you suspect a double-brokered load

  • Stop and call the broker using the phone number from its official listing, not the one in the email.
  • Ask the shipper who they released the load to.
  • Do not load until the names on the rate confirmation and the bill of lading match.
  • Report confirmed fraud to the FMCSA and to the load board where it was posted.

Frequently asked questions

Who is liable if I haul a double-brokered load?

It gets complicated and often ends with the carrier unpaid. Prevention is far cheaper than recovery, which is why verification before pickup matters.

Are new carriers targeted more often?

Yes. Scammers know new carriers are hungry for loads and less likely to verify. That is exactly when a careful dispatcher helps most.

Is a high rate always a scam?

No. Hot loads and tight markets pay more. A high rate is a reason to verify, not a reason to refuse.
