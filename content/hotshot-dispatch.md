Hotshot trucking uses a heavy-duty pickup, usually a 3500, 4500 or 5500, pulling a gooseneck or bumper-pull flatbed trailer. Hotshots move smaller, time-sensitive loads that cannot wait for a full-size flatbed: a machine part for a stopped plant, equipment for a job site, or supplies for an oilfield crew.

## What hotshot loads look like

- Machinery parts and equipment that need to arrive fast
- Construction materials for job sites
- Oilfield and energy equipment
- Agricultural equipment and supplies
- Partial flatbed loads that do not fill a semi

## Weight is everything

Hotshot freight is limited by weight more than space. Your trailer length, the truck's payload and your total combined weight rating decide which loads you can legally take. We ask for your empty weight and ratings up front, and never book a load that would put you over.

> If your truck and trailer together are rated above 26,000 pounds combined, the driver needs a CDL. Many hotshot operators run a setup rated at or below that line to stay non-CDL, which limits how much they can haul.

## What we confirm before booking

- **Weight and dimensions** of the freight against your payload and deck length
- **Loading method** and whether you need to bring straps, chains or tarps
- **Delivery deadline**, since hotshot loads are often urgent and priced for speed
- **Site access** at job sites and rural locations

## A typical hotshot setup

The most common setup is a dually pickup, often a 3500, 4500 or 5500, pulling a 40-foot gooseneck flatbed. Shorter goosenecks and bumper-pull trailers carry less but cost less to run. Your truck's payload and combined weight rating matter more than trailer length, because they decide how heavy a load you can legally take.

## Keeping hotshot profitable

- **Combine partials** when two loads fit your weight and deck limits and run the same direction.
- **Plan backhauls** before you deliver, since hotshot lanes can be one-directional.
- **Track tires, fuel and maintenance** per mile; light-duty trucks wear faster under constant heavy loads.
- **Stay reachable**, because urgent hotshot loads go to whoever answers first.

## Frequently asked questions

### Do I need a USDOT number for hotshot?
If your truck, or truck and trailer combined, is rated at 10,001 pounds or more and you haul across state lines for hire, you need a USDOT number and operating authority.

### Is hotshot profitable?
It can be, especially for urgent loads that pay a premium. Fuel costs are lower than a semi, but loads are smaller and rates swing with the energy and construction markets. Good dispatch keeps empty miles low.

### Can you dispatch a bumper-pull setup?
Yes. Bumper-pull trailers carry less than goosenecks, so we filter loads to your trailer's limits.

### What trailer length is most common for hotshot?
A 40-foot gooseneck is the most common, but 30 to 36-foot goosenecks and bumper pulls are used too. We filter loads to your exact trailer.

### Can I haul two partial loads at once?
Often, yes, if the combined weight and space fit your setup and both brokers agree. We coordinate the pickups and deliveries.
