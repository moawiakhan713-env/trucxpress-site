Most truck dispatchers charge a percentage of each load, usually between 5% and 10% of the load rate. Others charge a flat weekly fee per truck or a fixed fee per load. On our sales calls, price is the first question owner-operators ask, so here is the full picture.

## The three ways dispatchers charge

| Pricing model | Typical range | How it is paid |
|---|---|---|
| Percentage of the load | About 5% to 10% | On each booked load |
| Flat weekly fee | Often a few hundred dollars per truck per week | Every week, booked or not |
| Per-load fee | A fixed amount per load | On each booked load |

Ranges vary by equipment and by what the service includes. Box truck, hotshot and specialized freight can price differently from dry van.

## What a fair price should include

A dispatcher earning 5% to 10% should be doing more than posting your truck on a load board. Expect:

- Load searching and rate negotiation on every load
- Broker vetting for authority, bond and payment history
- Setup packets and rate confirmation review
- Check calls and broker updates
- Planning reloads to reduce empty miles
- Help with paperwork for invoicing or factoring

## Hidden fees to ask about

- **Setup or onboarding fees**
- **Software or technology fees**, weekly or monthly
- **Per-packet fees** for each broker setup
- **Factoring referral arrangements** that raise your factoring cost
- **Percentage on the gross**, including fuel surcharge and accessorials, instead of the line-haul rate

Add every fee together and compare the total, not just the headline percentage.

## Example: what a percentage looks like in a real week

Say your truck books four loads this week for a total of $7,200. At 7%, the dispatch fee is $504. If the dispatcher negotiated even $100 more on each load than you would have gotten alone, that is $400 back. These are example numbers, but they show why the rate a dispatcher books matters as much as the fee.

## Red flags in dispatcher pricing

- Payment required before any load is booked
- A long contract with penalties for leaving
- A dispatcher who will not tell you the full rate on the rate confirmation
- Pressure to use a specific factoring company without explaining why

## Frequently asked questions

### Is a cheaper dispatcher always better?
No. A dispatcher who negotiates $0.20 more per mile pays for a higher percentage many times over. Compare rates booked, not just fees.

### Do dispatchers charge on loads I find myself?
A good one should not. Ask before signing. TrucXpress only charges on loads we book.

### Can I try a dispatcher without a contract?
With TrucXpress, yes. There is no long-term contract, so the service has to earn your business every week.

### Should I see the broker's rate confirmation?
Yes, always. You are the carrier, and the rate confirmation is your contract with the broker. A dispatcher who hides it is a red flag.
