Our office is in Phoenix, so we see the same pattern every week: dry vans deliver into Arizona at a good rate, then sit at a truck stop wondering why everything going out pays so little. Here is how we plan around it.

## Why outbound Arizona pays less

Phoenix is a large, growing consumer market. It takes in far more freight than it ships out, so there are more trucks looking to leave than loads leaving. Brokers know it, and outbound offers reflect it. Inbound rates are usually the strong half of an Arizona round trip.

## Plan the exit before you enter

The most expensive move in Arizona is delivering on a Friday afternoon with no plan. We start looking for the outbound load as soon as you accept the inbound one. That gives us days instead of hours to find a fair rate.

## Strategies that work

1. **Triangle routes.** Instead of a straight Phoenix out-and-back, run a loop through stronger markets. A loaded run into Arizona, a load toward Texas or Southern California, and a better-paying load back keeps the truck earning on every leg.
2. **Short repositioning.** When Phoenix outbound is weak, a short move to a market with more outbound freight can pay off over the week. We compare that move against waiting for a local load.
3. **Tucson and the I-10 corridor.** Freight around Tucson and along I-10 sometimes offers options when the Phoenix board is thin.
4. **Seasonal timing.** In winter, produce volume out of Nogales and Yuma pulls reefer capacity away, which can lift rates for dry vans on some outbound lanes.

## Judge the round trip, not the leg

A cheap outbound load is not automatically a bad deal if the inbound paid well and the outbound puts you in a strong market for the next load. We measure the whole loop: total pay divided by total miles, including deadhead.

## A sample loop

Here is an illustration, not a rate quote. A truck delivers a well-paid load into Phoenix on Tuesday. Instead of waiting for a cheap Phoenix outbound, we book a short load to a stronger nearby market for Wednesday, then a good-paying load back toward the carrier's home region on Thursday. The middle leg pays less than the others, but the week earns more than sitting in Phoenix for two days.

## Mistakes we see in Arizona

- Accepting a Friday delivery into Phoenix with no plan for the weekend
- Taking the first outbound load because it is there, even when it heads into another weak market
- Ignoring summer heat, which is hard on tires, coolant and drivers
- Forgetting that dust storms can close parts of I-10 between Phoenix and Tucson

## Frequently asked questions

### What day is best to leave Phoenix?
Midweek usually has more outbound options than Friday afternoons and weekends, when brokers and shippers slow down.

### Should I refuse loads into Phoenix?
No. Inbound Phoenix loads often pay well. Just plan the way out before you commit.

### Do you dispatch carriers based outside Arizona?
Yes. We dispatch across the United States, and we use our local knowledge whenever a carrier's route touches Arizona.

### Does summer heat affect dry van loads?
It mostly affects the truck and driver: tires, cooling systems and hours spent at docks in the heat. Some shippers move temperature-sensitive goods into reefers in summer, which can shift dry van volume.
