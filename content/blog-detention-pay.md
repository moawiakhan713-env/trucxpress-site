Waiting at a dock is unpaid driving time unless you claim it. Detention pay compensates carriers when a shipper or receiver keeps the truck longer than the free time allowed. Many carriers lose this money simply because nobody documented the wait. On our desk, detention is something we plan for before the load starts.

## How detention usually works

- **Free time:** commonly two hours at pickup and two hours at delivery.
- **Hourly rate after free time:** set by the broker or shipper, often in the range of tens of dollars per hour.
- **Approval:** the broker must approve it, and it is usually paid when the load is paid.

Terms vary by broker and shipper, so the rate confirmation decides what you actually get.

## Six steps that get detention approved

1. **Get the terms on the rate confirmation.** Free time, hourly rate and any cap. If it is not written, it is hard to collect.
2. **Arrive on time.** Detention usually starts from your appointment time, not when you arrive early. Being late can cancel it.
3. **Record your times.** Check-in and check-out times on the bill of lading, signed by the facility when possible. Your ELD timestamps and photos back it up.
4. **Warn the broker early.** Call or message before free time runs out, not after. Brokers can push the facility, and early notice is often required to approve detention.
5. **Send the paperwork promptly** with the invoice: signed BOL with times, and any lumper or other receipts.
6. **Follow up.** Unclaimed detention is the easiest money a carrier leaves behind.

## Related charges to know

- **Layover:** paid when a delay forces you to wait overnight or longer.
- **TONU (truck ordered, not used):** paid when a load is cancelled after you were dispatched or arrived.
- **Driver assist and lumper:** for unloading help or paid lumpers, reimbursed with a receipt.

## Detention in numbers

Here is an example. Your rate confirmation says two hours free and $50 per hour after that. You arrive on time for an 8:00 appointment and leave at 12:30. That is 4.5 hours at the dock, 2 free, so 2.5 billable hours, or $125 if the broker approves it. Without timestamps and an early warning call, that $125 usually disappears.

## Facilities that hold trucks

Some shippers and receivers are known for long waits. Dispatchers and drivers remember them. When a load picks up or delivers at one, we either negotiate the rate up, insist on detention terms, or pass on it.

## Frequently asked questions

### Does detention start when I arrive?
Usually it starts at the appointment time, not your arrival, and only after free time has passed. Check your rate confirmation.

### What if the facility will not sign my times?
Note the times yourself on the BOL, take photos, and rely on your ELD records. Tell the broker right away.

### Can a dispatcher help collect detention?
Yes. At TrucXpress we negotiate detention terms before booking, alert the broker as free time runs out, and include the documents with your invoice.

### Is detention paid for drop and hook loads?
Usually not in the same way, since you are not waiting to be loaded. If a drop trailer is not ready and you wait, report it right away and ask for detention under your rate confirmation terms.
