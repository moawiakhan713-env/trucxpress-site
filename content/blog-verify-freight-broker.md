A load is only as good as the broker who pays for it. Our dispatch desk runs the same checks before every booking, and they take about ten minutes. Here is the process, step by step.

## Step 1: Confirm broker authority with FMCSA

Search the broker's MC or USDOT number on the FMCSA's public records. Check that:

- The authority type includes **broker**, not only carrier.
- The authority status is **active**, not pending, revoked or inactive.
- The company name matches the name on the rate confirmation exactly.

## Step 2: Check the bond

Freight brokers must keep a $75,000 bond or trust fund on file with FMCSA, which is meant to cover carriers if a broker does not pay. The FMCSA licensing and insurance records show whether that bond is active. A cancelled or pending bond is a reason to walk away.

## Step 3: Look at credit and payment history

Load board credit scores, factoring company checks and days-to-pay data show whether a broker pays on time. If your factoring company refuses to buy a broker's invoices, treat that as a strong warning.

## Step 4: Verify the contact, not just the company

Fraudsters often pose as real brokers. Compare:

- The **phone number** you were given with the one on the official FMCSA listing or the broker's real website
- The **email domain** with the broker's actual domain, letter by letter
- The **address** on the rate confirmation with the official listing

When in doubt, call the broker using the number from the official listing and confirm the load.

## Step 5: Read the rate confirmation

Make sure the rate, pickup and delivery details, detention terms and payment terms match what you agreed. Names on the rate confirmation and later the bill of lading should match the broker you booked with.

> If any step fails, do not haul until it is resolved. A cheap load you skip costs far less than a load you never get paid for.

## Free tools that do most of the work

- **FMCSA public records** for authority status and contact details
- **FMCSA licensing and insurance records** for the broker bond
- **Load board credit scores** and days-to-pay data
- **Your factoring company's credit check** on the broker

None of these cost extra if you already use a load board or factor, and together they catch most problems.

## If you think you have been scammed

1. Stop any further pickups with that broker.
2. Contact the shipper and the real broker, if one exists, to report what happened.
3. Gather the rate confirmation, emails, phone numbers and bills of lading.
4. File a complaint with the FMCSA and report the posting to the load board.
5. If you factor, alert your factoring company immediately.

## Frequently asked questions

### What is a BMC-84 bond?
It is the $75,000 surety bond brokers file with FMCSA. A trust fund filing, called BMC-85, is the alternative. Either way, an active filing is a basic requirement.

### Is a broker with a new authority automatically a scam?
No. New brokers start every day. A new authority just means you should check everything else more carefully.

### What if the broker's email looks right?
Look closely. Scammers use domains that differ by one letter or add a word. Call the official number to confirm.

### How long does verification take?
With practice, about ten minutes per new broker. Repeat brokers you already work with need only a quick check that nothing has changed.
