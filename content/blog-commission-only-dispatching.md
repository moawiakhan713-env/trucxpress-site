Starting a trucking company is expensive enough: authority, insurance, a truck note, fuel. When I started TrucXpress, the thing I heard most from new carriers was that they had paid a dispatch service before a single load was booked, and then sat empty anyway. Commission-only dispatching exists to fix exactly that.

## The problem with paying before you earn

Plenty of dispatch services charge setup fees, weekly flat rates or monthly subscriptions. For a new MC the math is brutal. You pay while your cash is tightest, and the service gets paid **whether or not it performs**. A flat-fee dispatcher who books you nothing this week still collects.

## How commission-only dispatching works

You pay a percentage of each load that is actually booked and hauled. No load, no fee. That gives you:

- **Zero risk to start.** No setup cost while money is tight.
- **Aligned incentives.** Your dispatcher only earns when you earn, so they fight for better-paying freight.
- **No lock-in.** Without a subscription, the service has to prove its value every single week.

## Why it matters most for a new authority

New authorities face a chicken-and-egg problem. Many brokers hesitate to load an MC that is only a few weeks old, and without loads you cannot build a track record. A commission-only dispatcher shares that problem with you, because they do not get paid until they solve it. At TrucXpress we build broker lists for new MCs, handle the setup packets, and choose first loads that build your history. We wrote a full guide on [dispatch for new authority](dispatch-for-new-authority.html).

## What to check in any commission deal

1. **What the percentage is calculated on**: the line-haul rate, or the total including fuel and extra charges.
2. **Whether there are other fees** on top: setup, software, packet or factoring referral fees.
3. **Whether you can refuse a load.** You should always have the final say.
4. **How you leave.** Short notice, no penalty.

## The bottom line

Before you sign with any dispatch service, ask one question: "What happens if you book me nothing this week?" If the answer is "you still pay," keep looking. Performance-based dispatching keeps your risk low and your dispatcher hungry, which is how it should be.

## What commission-only does not mean

- **It is not free.** You pay a percentage on every load booked for you.
- **It is not forced loads.** You still approve or refuse every load.
- **It is not always the cheapest option** in your strongest weeks. It is the option where you never pay for an empty week. See our [percentage vs flat fee](blog-dispatch-percentage-vs-flat-fee.html) breakdown.

## Frequently asked questions

### What percentage do commission dispatchers usually charge?
Most charge somewhere between 5% and 10% of the load rate, depending on equipment and services included. TrucXpress quotes your exact percentage in writing.

### Is commission-only dispatching good for one-truck owner-operators?
Yes. It is often the best fit, because a single truck has the least cash cushion for fixed weekly fees in a slow week.

### Can I combine my own loads with dispatched loads?
Yes. You only pay commission on loads the dispatcher books for you.
