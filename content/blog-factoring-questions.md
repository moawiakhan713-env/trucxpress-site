Brokers often pay 30 days or more after delivery. For a small carrier with fuel, insurance and a truck note due every week, that wait can be the difference between running and parking. Factoring solves the wait by buying your invoices for a fee. Signed carelessly, it becomes one of the most expensive decisions a new carrier makes.

## How factoring works

You deliver a load and send the paperwork to the factoring company. It advances most of the invoice to you, often within a day, then collects from the broker. When the broker pays, the factor keeps its fee and may release a remaining reserve to you.

## The 8 questions to ask

1. **What is the total fee, and how is it calculated?** Many factors charge a few percent per invoice. Ask whether it is flat or rises the longer the broker takes to pay.
2. **Is it recourse or non-recourse?** With recourse, you owe the money back if the broker never pays. Non-recourse protects you only against certain cases, usually a broker's insolvency, not disputes.
3. **What is the advance rate and the reserve?** Some factors advance 100% minus the fee. Others hold back a reserve until the broker pays.
4. **Is there a minimum volume?** Monthly minimums penalize slow months and seasonal carriers.
5. **How long is the contract, and how do I leave?** Look for automatic renewals, termination fees and long notice periods.
6. **Do I have to factor every invoice?** Full-turnover contracts require you to factor all loads, even from brokers who pay fast.
7. **How do release letters work?** If you switch factors, you need a release letter so brokers pay the new one. Ask how fast they issue it.
8. **What other fees are there?** Same-day transfer fees, invoice fees, credit check fees and fuel advance fees can add up.

## Use the factor's credit checks

A good factoring company checks broker credit before you haul. Use it. If your factor will not buy invoices from a broker, that is valuable information about that broker.

## Make factoring paperwork painless

Clean paperwork gets paid fast: a signed rate confirmation, a clear bill of lading with delivery signature, and lumper or detention receipts. Our dispatchers coordinate with your factor so documents go out the same day you deliver.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is factoring a loan?
No. Factoring is the sale of an invoice, not a loan, though recourse agreements mean you can still owe money if a broker does not pay.

### Do I need factoring as a new carrier?
Not always. If you have enough cash to cover 30 to 45 days of expenses, you may skip it. Many new carriers use it for the first months and stop later.

### Will my dispatcher work with my factoring company?
Yes. We include your Notice of Assignment in setup packets so brokers pay the factor directly.

### Can I stop factoring later?
Usually, yes, if your contract allows it. Read the termination terms and notice period before signing, and make sure you will get release letters for your brokers.
