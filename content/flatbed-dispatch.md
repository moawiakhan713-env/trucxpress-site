Flatbed pays for skill. Loading, securing and tarping open-deck freight takes experience, and the rates reflect it. The money disappears fast, though, when a load turns out to need tarps nobody mentioned, an oversize permit, or securement gear you do not carry. Our dispatchers get those details before you say yes.

## Common flatbed freight

- Steel coils, pipe, beams and rebar
- Lumber and building materials
- Machinery and equipment
- Roofing, drywall and other construction supplies
- Solar panels, racking and energy project materials

## What we confirm before booking

- **Dimensions and weight:** loads wider than 8'6", taller than legal height or heavier than legal axle weights need permits and sometimes escorts.
- **Tarps:** whether the load must be tarped, which tarp size, and the tarp pay.
- **Securement:** chains, straps, edge protectors and coil racks the shipper expects you to bring, in line with federal cargo securement rules.
- **Loading method:** forklift, crane or side load, and how long loading usually takes.
- **Site access:** job sites can have tight entries, soft ground and limited hours.

## Rates that account for the work

A flatbed rate should cover more than miles. We negotiate tarp pay, detention, extra stops and oversize charges separately, so the effort you put into a heavy or complicated load shows up on the rate confirmation.

## Tarping basics

Tarps protect freight from weather and road grime, and they are hard, physical work. The common types are lumber tarps for tall loads, steel tarps with shorter drops for coils and low freight, and smoke tarps that cover the front of a load. We confirm the tarp type and size before booking, so you do not show up with the wrong tarps or find out at the shipper that the load needs tarping you were never paid for.

## Flatbed seasons and lanes

Flatbed demand follows construction. Spring through fall is the busy stretch across most of the country, while winter slows building in the northern states. Steel and metals move out of the Midwest and the Gulf Coast, lumber out of the Southeast and Northwest, and solar and energy projects keep the Southwest busy. We plan your weeks around where open-deck freight is moving, so you are not stuck in a market where building has paused.

## Frequently asked questions

### Do you book oversize loads?
We book oversize loads when your truck and experience fit them. We confirm who arranges and pays for permits and escorts before you commit.

### My trailer is a 48-foot flatbed. Is that a problem?
No. Many flatbed loads fit a 48-foot deck. We filter loads by deck length and weight limit.

### Is a step deck better than a flatbed?
They serve different loads. A step deck carries taller freight within legal height. See our [step deck dispatch](step-deck-dispatch.html) page for details.

### What securement gear should I carry?
Most flatbedders carry chains and binders, 4-inch straps with winches, edge and corner protectors, coil racks or pads, dunnage, and a full set of tarps. Specific loads can need more, so we confirm requirements with the shipper first.

### Does tarp pay vary?
Yes. Tarp pay is usually a separate charge per load and varies by broker, load and tarp size. We negotiate it as its own line on the rate confirmation.
