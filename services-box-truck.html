Box Truck Dispatch Service

Box trucks are one of the fastest-growing parts of trucking. They get into city streets, small docks and job sites that a 53-foot trailer cannot reach, and many operate without a commercial driver's license. The challenge is that box truck loads are scattered across many small brokers and expedite networks. A dispatcher who knows where to look makes the difference.

What box trucks haul

  • Expedited freight: time-sensitive parts and shipments that cannot wait for a full truckload.
  • Partial and LTL-size loads: a few pallets that do not need a full trailer.
  • Final mile and residential delivery: furniture, appliances and retail goods, often needing a lift gate.
  • Regional runs: repeat routes between warehouses and stores.

Details we confirm before booking

  • Truck length and payload: most box trucks run 16 to 26 feet, carrying around 10 to 14 pallets on the larger sizes. We match pallet count and weight to your truck.
  • Lift gate and pallet jack: many deliveries have no dock, so the load needs a lift gate and sometimes a pallet jack.
  • Dock height and access: some receivers only accept dock-height trucks. City deliveries can have time windows and parking limits.
  • Team or solo: expedited loads sometimes need team drivers to meet the delivery time.

Paperwork box truck owners often miss

If you haul for hire across state lines, you need a USDOT number and operating authority, just like a semi. Brokers will also ask for insurance with cargo coverage. Most box trucks with a gross vehicle weight rating of 26,000 pounds or less can be driven without a CDL, unless you haul placarded hazardous materials.

Building a strong box truck week

Box truck freight rewards responsiveness. Expedite loads often go to the first truck that answers, so staying reachable and having a dispatcher watching boards all day makes a real difference. Many box truck owners mix local or regional repeat work with longer expedited runs, which keeps the truck moving and brings the driver home more often.

Costs that work in your favor

Box trucks burn less fuel, cost less to insure than most tractor-trailers and are cheaper to maintain. That means a lower rate per mile can still be profitable, as long as empty miles stay low. We track your cost per mile and book loads that clear it.

Frequently asked questions

Do I need a CDL to drive a box truck?

Usually not, if the truck's gross vehicle weight rating is 26,000 pounds or less and you are not hauling placarded hazmat. Check your truck's rating on the door label.

Is a lift gate worth it?

For most box truck owners, yes. A lift gate opens up deliveries to stores, job sites and homes without docks, and those loads often pay extra.

Do box truck loads pay per mile like semis?

Many do, but rates per mile are usually lower than tractor-trailer rates, and expedited loads can pay a premium. We weigh rate, miles and time together.

Do you dispatch team box trucks for expedited loads?

Yes. Team drivers can take time-critical, long-distance expedited loads that solo drivers cannot complete within hours-of-service limits.
