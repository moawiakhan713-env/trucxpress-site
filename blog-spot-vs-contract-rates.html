Spot Rates vs Contract Rates: What Owner-Operators Should Know

Every load rate falls into one of two buckets: spot or contract. Knowing the difference helps you understand why rates move, when to hold out for more, and when a steady lane is worth a slightly lower number. Our sales team explains this on almost every call with a new carrier.

Spot rates

A spot rate is a one-time price for a single load, set by supply and demand that day. Most loads a small carrier books through load boards and brokers are spot loads.

  • Upside: when trucks are scarce in a market, spot rates jump and you capture it.
  • Downside: when trucks are plentiful, spot rates fall, sometimes below what it costs you to run.

Contract rates

Contract rates are agreed in advance, usually between a shipper and a carrier or broker for a set period. Large shippers run bids to lock in rates for their lanes.

  • Upside: predictable pay and repeat freight.
  • Downside: when the spot market rises, a contract rate can lag behind it. Shippers sometimes move freight to the spot market when contract carriers turn loads down.

Why spot rates swing

  • Season: produce, retail peaks, holidays and month-end shipping
  • Weather: storms that take trucks out of a region
  • Market direction: some cities ship far more than they receive, and the reverse
  • Capacity: carriers entering or leaving the market over months and years

How a small carrier can use both

  1. Keep a base of repeat lanes from reliable brokers or direct shippers, so you are never starting from zero.
  2. Fill the gaps with spot loads when the market is strong, and stay picky when it is weak.
  3. Track your cost per mile so you know the lowest rate that still makes money.
  4. Do not chase every spike into a market with nothing coming back.

Know your cost per mile

You cannot judge any rate without knowing your break-even. Add up a month of costs:

  • Fixed: truck payment, insurance, permits, phone and software
  • Variable: fuel, maintenance, tires, tolls

Divide the total by the miles you ran that month, including empty miles. If the truck costs $1.70 per mile to run and a load pays $1.90 per total mile, that load earns 20 cents a mile. Knowing this number lets you say no with confidence.

Frequently asked questions

Can owner-operators get contract freight?

Yes, often through brokers with dedicated lanes or by building relationships with local shippers. It usually takes some history and reliable service first.

Is it better to run spot or contract?

Most small carriers do best with a mix: repeat freight for stability and spot loads to capture strong markets.

How does a dispatcher help with rates?

A dispatcher watches rates on your lanes every day, negotiates each load and knows when to wait for a better offer.

Where can I see current spot rates?

Load boards publish lane rate averages, and many industry sites post weekly market updates. Your dispatcher also sees what brokers are paying on your lanes every day.
